PERSONNEL DATABASE

Alexander Dubček (Reform Communist)

Position: First Secretary of the Communist Party of Czechoslovakia (KSČ).

Ideology: Dubček was a loyal communist, but he realized the Soviet economic model was failing. He believed communism could be "fixed" from within. He coined the phrase "Socialism with a human face."

Goals: He wanted to end extreme state censorship, allow basic freedom of speech, and improve the economy. However, he did not want a multi-party democracy and he constantly promised the Soviets that Czechoslovakia would never leave the Warsaw Pact military alliance.

Leonid Brezhnev (Orthodox Communist)

Position: General Secretary of the Soviet Union.

Ideology: A strict hardliner. He believed that the Communist Party must maintain absolute, unquestioned monopoly over every aspect of life in the Eastern Bloc. He viewed free speech as a "bourgeois" disease that led to capitalism.

Goals: To maintain the "buffer zone" of Eastern Europe at all costs. He ultimately formulated the Brezhnev Doctrine, declaring the USSR had the right to use military force against any socialist country that tried to reform its system.

Radical Students & Intellectuals (Grassroots Democracy)

Position: Citizens, writers (like Ludvík Vaculík), and university students.

Ideology: They believed Dubček's reforms did not go far enough. Once Dubček removed censorship, these groups used the newspapers to demand massive, rapid change.

Goals: They wanted true democracy: the creation of rival political parties, free elections independent of the Communist Party, and a complete separation from Soviet influence. Both Dubček and Brezhnev saw them as a major threat to state control.
